# Versalius Mundi — Season 1 Shooting Schedule
*Companion to `episode-plan.md`. Two producers, no prior episodes. Day 1 = the Monday Tom says go.*

**First post: Day 12 (Friday, week 2).**
**First pilot cut: week 11. Locked cut: week 13. Pilot live: week 14.**

Everything below is sized so that a short never costs more than three working days, and the pilot gets the rest.

---

## The first four weeks in full

### Week 1 — Build the shop (no posts)

The whole week is spent making one short, not twelve. The template is the asset; every later week reuses it.

**Alex**
- Lock the short template: three beats — Line 1 opener, the proof, the close line. 1080×1920, burned-in captions, GDN lockup on the last frame. Build it once as a reusable project file.
- Fix the bench set: backdrop, light, camera height, where Tom stands. Photograph it so it can be rebuilt identically in week 6.
- Shoot batch one — **S1, S2, S3** — in one session with Tom. Two takes each, plus one spare line per short for the edit.
- Export preset: one master file per short, named `S01_first-repair_master.mp4`. Same file goes to all four platforms.

**Lauren**
- Claim the same handle on TikTok, YouTube, Instagram and Facebook. Same name, same profile photo, same one-line bio.
- One link-in-bio page pointing at the GDN site, with an email capture field. Nothing else on it.
- Build the tracker: episode, post date, platform, views, saves/shares, comments, link clicks, email signups.
- Write the reply routine and the four platform captions for S1.

**Tom**
- Approve the template and the first four briefs. Approve the handle/name.
- Sit for the batch-one shoot.

**End of week 1:** template locked, three shorts in the can, S1 in the edit.

---

### Week 2 — First post

**Alex**
- Edit S1, then S2. Deliver each to Lauren **48 hours before** its post day — this is the rule that keeps the rest of the season from slipping.
- Shoot S4 and S5 if the bench is still standing; if not, they move to week 3.

**Lauren**
- Post **S1 across all four platforms on the same day — Day 12.** Native upload to each; one file, four posts.
- Sit on the comments for the first hour after posting and reply to every one. That hour is most of the reach.
- Email the list once S1 is up, one short paragraph, one link.
- Log day-one numbers in the tracker that evening.

**Tom**
- Final look at S1 before it posts. Approve or name the one fix — not a re-edit.

**End of week 2:** one short live on four platforms, second short finished and waiting.

---

### Week 3 — Get the rhythm

**Alex**
- Edit S3. Shoot S4–S5.
- Confirm the export/caption process is repeatable — if week 2 took four days, cut something now, not in week 9.

**Lauren**
- Post **S2 on Day 19.** Same routine: post day, first hour on comments, log that night.
- First one-page read to Tom: what held, what got saved or shared, what got skipped, any comment worth answering in a short.

**End of week 3:** two shorts live, two in the can, a working weekly rhythm.

---

### Week 4 — Read the numbers, start the pilot

**Alex**
- **Pilot work begins.** Board the six pilot beats against the spine lines (the pilot's Line 3 gap gets fixed here, at board stage). Rough animatic: stills, temp voice, no finished animation. Target: animatic done by end of week 7.
- Still ships one short this week — the pilot does not get to starve the shorts track.

**Lauren**
- Post **S3 on Day 26.**
- Read the first three shorts together: which spine line travelled furthest. That read does not reorder the Foundations pass (it walks the spine in order) — it decides which line gets the stronger treatment in the Proof pass.
- Draft the pilot launch list: what the email says, which platform gets it first, what gets pinned.

**Tom**
- Read the three-week numbers. Approve the Proof pass briefs (S7–S12).
- Approve the pilot animatic when it lands, week 7.

**End of week 4:** three shorts live, Foundations pass half-delivered, pilot boarded.

**Why the pilot is not cut before week 11:** a 3–4 minute animated piece is the single biggest item either producer has ever made. Cutting it first and the shorts second would cost the posting rhythm, which is the one thing that can't be bought back later. Shorts ship weekly; the pilot gets the leftover hours and then a clean run in weeks 12–13.

## What slips, and in what order

If a week goes badly, the order of sacrifice is fixed so nobody has to improvise at 11pm:

1. **Drop the second take** — post the short with the simpler edit.
2. **Skip a week of posting** — one skipped week is recoverable; a rushed pilot is not.
3. **Cut pilot scope, not pilot quality** — fewer montage shots in Beat C, more text cards. The margin beat and Beat D's comparison never get cut.
4. **Never** ship a number that didn't come from Reid, or a short that breaks a never-on-screen rule to save a day.

## Two producers, eight jobs — the split

**Alex:** brief to board, shoot, animate, edit, captions, export the single 9:16 master, deliver 48h ahead.
**Lauren:** four-platform posting, first-hour comment replies, link-in-bio and email capture, tracker, weekly one-page read to Tom, pilot launch.

Neither posts the other's work. Neither promises a figure that isn't on the brief. Tom approves every brief before it's shot and every finished short before it posts.
