# Versalius Mundi — Season 1 Episode Plan
*Built from `message-spine.md` (Tom approved). Every episode names the spine line it lands.*
*Producers: Alex (design + video) and Lauren (funnel + comms). Neither has made an episode before — this plan is sized for that.*

---

## The shape

**One season = 12 shorts + 1 flagship pilot, over 14 weeks.**

- **Shorts track — discovery.** 9:16, 30–60 seconds, one master file posted to TikTok, YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels and Facebook. One spine line per short. Made to be understood with the sound off and with no prior knowledge of the series.
- **Pilot — flagship, separate.** "The Long Defense," 3–4 minutes, 9:16. Carries all six spine lines. Published as the season finale, not as episode one of the shorts.

**Why two tracks and not one:** the shorts earn attention weekly; the pilot is the thing worth arriving for. If the pilot went first, it would be one long video on a channel nobody follows yet, and it would spend the whole wow on an audience of strangers. The shorts build the audience that the pilot lands on.

---

## Two house rules

**1. Every short opens on Line 1.** The spine says start every episode at "the first repair is the decision." So each short gets one opening beat of Line 1 — one sentence, same every week — then lands its own named line. That makes twelve shorts feel like a series instead of twelve unrelated posts, and it trains the audience on the one idea everything else hangs from.

**2. The wow is budgeted.** Tom's call: momentum early, wow saved for the pilot.

| Weeks | Pass | What it spends |
|---|---|---|
| 2–7 | **Foundations** (shorts 1–6) | Tom's voice, macro stills, text cards. No animation, no characters. |
| 8–13 | **Proof** (shorts 7–12) | One animated element per short — the margin glow, a gap opening, a chain forming. Still no characters. |
| 14 | **Pilot** | The whole character world debuts here: Aurea Puella (Aureal), Compo, sePHen, TOOB, Masseter. First appearance, all of it. Cast per `world-bible.md`. |

Nothing in the shorts track uses a character before week 14. The first time anyone meets Aureal is in the pilot. That is the wow, and it is not spent on a Tuesday.

## Season 1 — the shorts

Runtime is a target, not a limit. Close line is the last thing on screen.

### Foundations pass (weeks 2–7)

**S1 · "The First Repair"** — lands **Line 1**. Post week 2.
The first filling is not a filling. It is a decision made once, on a tooth that was whole, by someone who trusted us. Most first cavities are on back teeth — outside the smile, hardest to clean, and the patient can't watch them.
*On screen:* Tom at the bench, one line each; cut to macro of a back tooth; two text cards. 30–40s.
*Number:* none required. (Optional "1 in 4 adults with untreated decay" — Reid supplies the wording or it runs without.)
*Close:* "You only get one first repair."

**S2 · "The Leak, Not the Break"** — lands **Line 2**. Post week 3.
Restorations don't fail by breaking. They fail at the edge. Composite shrinks as it cures and pulls away from the tooth, and new decay gets in at the edge.
*Number:* Reid supplies (replacements vs. new placements; secondary caries as the leading reason for replacement) — or the short runs with no number.
*Close:* "A crack is an invitation."

**S3 · "Rock, Paper, Scissors"** — lands **Line 3**. Post week 4.
A restoration must never be harder than the tooth it chews against. Ceramic doesn't wear with the tooth — it wears the tooth opposite. Gold sits at enamel hardness and wears with it. No dental degree needed for this one.
*Number:* Reid supplies the hardness comparison, or the line stands on the plain statement.
*Close:* "Hardness has to match."

**S4 · "No Code for Gold"** — lands **Line 4**. Post week 5.
There is no CDT code for a gold inlay. No code means no reimbursement, so patients can't afford it and dentists stop offering it. The gap is in the system, not in the dentist.
*Number:* **none.** No contract terms, no percentages, no thresholds. Tom-reported figures are not cleared for screen.
*Close:* "It is a missing choice."

**S5 · "Especially Now"** — lands **Line 5**. Post week 6.
Everyone prices gold by the ounce. The number that matters is the re-treatments it prevents. The audience is in a money pinch and angry at insurers — meet them there with the math, not with fear.
*Number:* ceiling is "two to three re-treatments avoided." **No dollar figures.** The 30-year model is not cleared.
*Close:* "Cheap is the expensive one."

**S6 · "Still in Service"** — lands **Line 6**. Post week 7.
Gold placed before the patient's dentist was born is still doing its job. And it's fixable — a code, a documented case, a conversation. End on the patient holding the choice, never on alarm.
*Number:* Reid supplies the in-service range and survival rate, or the claim runs without one.
*Close:* "They are yours, not the insurance company's."

### Proof pass (weeks 8–13)

Same three-beat shape, one animated element each. This is where the craft visibly steps up — the audience should be able to feel the difference between week 2 and week 9.

**S7 · "72.7%"** — lands **Line 1**. Post week 8.
A whole country swapped the material, and the failure they got was the margin. Real-world population, real-world result — the caries anchor, not an attack on anyone.
*Number:* Reid supplies, with the exact wording rule attached (the survey ran about a year after the ban; published 2016 — never "ten years later").
*Close:* "The material changed. The failure moved to the edge."

**S8 · "The Gap You Can't See"** — lands **Line 2**. Post week 9.
First use of the gold glow — margin zone only. A gap opens and stays open, and something has to fill it. This is the short that plants the pilot's battlefield in the audience's head.
*Number:* Reid supplies, with the corroboration-only rule attached (trade press: quote as reinforcement, never as our basis).
*Close:* "That space has to be filled with something."

**S9 · "The Maker's Own Warning"** — lands **Line 3**. Post week 10.
The ceramic maker's own instructions warn against using it in patients who grind or clench. That is not us saying it. That is the label.
*Number:* wording must be confirmed before it goes on screen, or it is paraphrased as "the maker's own instructions warn against it."
*Close:* "The instructions say so."

**S10 · "The Cascade"** — lands **Line 4**. Post week 11.
One animated chain: no code → no reimbursement → patients can't afford it → dentists stop offering it → schools stop teaching it → a generation graduates never having placed one. Never attach a statistic to any link.
*Number:* **none.**
*Close:* "A policy failure, not a clinical judgment."

**S11 · "Two or Three"** — lands **Line 5**. Post week 12.
The ladder, animated: filling, bigger filling, crown, root canal, implant. Each rung removes more tooth than the one before. Say what it costs in tooth, not in dollars.
*Number:* ceiling "two to three re-treatments avoided." **No dollars.**
*Close:* "The second repair costs more than the first."

**S12 · "Ask for the Material"** — lands **Line 6**. Post week 13. Season closer.
Milled gold now hits the margin better and more consistently than cast, so the practical objection is gone. Ask what the options are. Name the material. Own the tooth. This one points at the pilot.
*Number:* Reid supplies the milled-vs-cast gap figures, or it runs as the plain claim.
*Close:* "Ask. Then own the tooth." — final card: **The Long Defense, next week.**

## The pilot — "The Long Defense" (week 14)

**Format:** 3–4 minutes, 9:16, the animated character world. Script v1 at `scripts/PILOT_EPISODE_The_Long_Defense.txt` is **SUPERSEDED** — its cast (Martha, Tech Sycophant, Dr. Chen, Ledger King, instrument cameos) is not Tom's. The pilot needs rewriting against `world-bible.md`. The beat shape below survives; the named characters in it do not.

**It is not short 13.** It is a separate flagship: longer, character-led, and it carries the entire spine in one arc.

| Beat | What it does | Spine line |
|---|---|---|
| A — The patient arrives | Martha, 68, two fillings, two stories | 1 |
| B — The flashback | Composite placed, the option never offered | 1, 4 |
| C — The margin war | Thirty years at the edge; the gap only widened | 2 |
| D — Two fillings, two stories | Gold, thirty years, margin pristine | 2, 6 |
| E — The dentist's choice | The comparison that matters; the margin contract | 4, 5 |
| F — Close | Patient as commander; end on agency | 6 |

Line 3 (hardness) is the pilot's weakest landing. **Fix at animatic stage:** give it four seconds in Beat C — the opposing tooth wearing while the gold wears with it. Cheap to board, expensive to add later.

**Brand discipline for the pilot:** dark Versalius theme, Bangers display type, gold glow in the margin zone only — never full-tooth, never decorative. Per the brand guide, that theme is for Versalius material; the shorts carry GDN branding instead.

**The pilot's job after it posts:** it is the thing the shorts point at. Every short from S12 forward ends on it. Lauren pins it, and it becomes the pinned post on all four platforms.

## Never on screen (carried from the spine)

No claim that caries is rising. No named carriers, manufacturers, schools or dentists — "Dentistry" / "Big Dental" only. No shame on patients or dentists. No number that isn't handed over by Reid from `spine-sources.md`. No gold for front teeth — concede anteriors to ceramic out loud, the argument is the back of the mouth. No manufactured urgency. The 30-year model's dollars and the consumables/silo insight stay off screen.

**Producer rule:** Alex and Lauren don't carry citations and don't invent numbers. A number arrives on the brief with its exact wording, or the episode runs without it.
