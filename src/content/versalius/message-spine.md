## The six lines

**1. Caries is the biggest disease there is, and the first repair is the decision.**
Not "a filling." A material choice made once, on a tooth that was whole, by a patient who trusted us — it decides whether that tooth is intact in ten, thirty, or fifty years. Most first cavities are on back teeth: outside the smile, hardest to clean, and the patient can't watch them. Start every episode here. When composite becomes the default, the thing that ends the restoration is new decay creeping in at the edge — and the dentists placing them already suspect the older material lasted longer.

**2. The margin is the battlefield.** *(Tom's priority #1)*
Restorations don't fail by breaking; they fail by leaking. New decay at the edge is the leading reason restorations get replaced. Composite shrinks as it cures and pulls away from the tooth, opening a gap. Ceramic doesn't shrink, but it needs a cushion of cement under it so the crown doesn't crack when it's seated — and that cushion is thick enough that the edge sits wide open, with a gap that has to be filled with something. Resin cement is food for the bacteria that cause decay; they break it down. Gold seats on a thin, even layer Tom mills to — metal against tooth, nothing there to eat. One comparison carries any episode: gold still in service after decades, at survival rates composite never reaches.

**3. Hardness has to match. A restoration must not be harder than the tooth it chews against.**
Ceramic runs harder than enamel and doesn't wear with the tooth — it wears the tooth opposite. The ceramic maker's own instructions say it shouldn't go in a mouth that grinds or clenches. Gold sits at enamel hardness and wears *with* the tooth. Rock-paper-scissors: no dental degree needed.

**4. The gap is in the system, not in the dentist.** *(Tom's priority #2)*
There is no CDT code for a gold inlay. Contracts treat cast restorations as a last resort: they downgrade them to a composite allowance, demand serious tooth loss before they'll cover one, limit how often it can be replaced, and exclude the wear repair that is gold's whole advantage. No code → no reimbursement → patients can't afford it → dentists stop offering it → schools stop teaching it → a generation graduates never having placed one. A policy failure, not a clinical judgment — and never put one dentist's skill on trial for it.

**5. Intrinsic value vs. inherent value — and "especially now" is economics.**
Everyone prices gold by the ounce. The number that matters is the re-treatments it prevents: a composite patient can expect several replacements over the same thirty years a gold patient never thinks about, each one removing more tooth — filling, bigger filling, crown, root canal, implant. The audience is in a money pinch and angry at insurers. Meet them there, with the math, not with fear.

**6. It's already proven, and it's fixable — so end on agency, never on alarm.**
Gold placed before the patient's dentist was born is still in service. Milled gold now hits the margin better and more consistently than cast, so the practical objection is gone. The fix is a code, a documented case, a conversation. End with the patient holding the choice: ask what the options are, name the material, own the tooth. *They are yours, not the insurance company's.*

## The tone

A technician for life — in the lab since 11, specializing in gold about thirty years, still at the bench. Plain declaratives, then the proof. Short aphoristic closers ("It is a missing choice." "Some will. They will tell others."). Calm about not convincing everyone — no urgency, no scare.

Talk to this generation as peers who happen to have molars. No nostalgia, no "kids today," no lecturing. Dry wit yes, heat no. Patients are sacrosanct; dentists are respected and never blamed. Never hide the science — meet scrutiny with material science and let the conclusion be theirs. Concede front teeth to ceramic out loud; the argument is the back of the mouth.

## Never on screen

- **No claim that caries is rising.** No "on a 30-year climb," "more decay than ever." That premise is a verified fault — it sits in the book drafts and must not reach the screen.
- **No named villains.** No carrier, manufacturer, school, or dentist by name — "Dentistry" / "Big Dental" generically. Ledger King stays an archetype; Tech Sycophant stays a role, never a person.
- **No shame** — not on patients, not on dentists. Never "you were duped."
- **No invented numbers.** Every figure on screen comes from `spine-sources.md`. If it isn't there, ask Tom — don't guess.
- **No gold for front teeth.** Back teeth only; ceramic where it's in the smile zone. No bashing without the fairness note — every material has its indication.
- **Not public yet:** the consumables/silo insight, and the 30-year model's dollar figures — all pending Tom's sign-off. Until he clears them, "two to three re-treatments avoided" is the ceiling.
- **No manufactured urgency.** No "act now," no deadlines, no "only N left."
- **Brand:** gold glow reserved for the margin zone only — never decorative, never the whole tooth.
